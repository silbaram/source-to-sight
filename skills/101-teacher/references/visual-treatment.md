# Visual treatment

Use this guide when the user has not supplied an aesthetic direction. Aim for a contemporary editorial explainer: clear, spacious, human, and quietly playful. Do not turn these preferences into a fixed template.

## Compose with intent

- Give the page one dominant visual idea and a clear reading rhythm. Prefer a strong hero, generous whitespace, and a few supporting callouts over a wall of equal cards.
- Use asymmetry or controlled overlap when it clarifies hierarchy. Keep alignment deliberate and preserve breathing room around labels, paths, and controls.
- Use a bento grid only when the content is genuinely modular and peers deserve equal weight. Avoid the generic dashboard look for a narrative lesson.

## Let type do real work

- Pair expressive display typography with calm, highly readable body text. Create hierarchy through scale, weight, width, and spacing before adding containers.
- Keep effects on type rare and purposeful. Never make the reader wait for text to settle, and do not animate body copy.

## Draw flows as flows

- For movement, exchange, cycles, and handoffs, favor continuous paths with smooth curves, generous bend radii, and rounded joins. Route them through whitespace and make every endpoint visibly connect.
- Use straight or orthogonal lines when they better express a boundary, measurement, comparison, or rigid system. Curves are semantic tools, not mandatory decoration.
- Avoid making every relationship the same rounded arrow. Preserve differences in direction, payload, trust, state, and outcome using the connector grammar in the diagram guide.

## Keep color and depth restrained

- Start from a quiet neutral base and a small semantic palette. One or two confident accents are often enough; add more only as a coherent system with a teaching purpose.
- Create depth with position, overlap, tonal contrast, and at most a light shadow or translucent layer. Maintain readable contrast and obvious boundaries.
- Avoid stock trend effects: decorative blob gradients, glass on every surface, glowing borders, gratuitous grain, and rounded rectangles around every sentence.

## Add motion where it earns attention

- Use short ease-out transitions, path-following markers, or state changes to guide attention and explain what happens. Keep most of the page still so meaningful motion is noticeable.
- Prefer `transform` and `opacity`, preserve the complete static explanation, and honor `prefers-reduced-motion`.

The result should feel authored for this subject rather than assembled from fashionable components. Accuracy, accessibility, and rendered QA outrank aesthetic novelty.
