# Visual treatment

Use this guide when the user has not supplied an aesthetic direction. Aim for a contemporary editorial explainer: clear, spacious, human, and quietly playful. Resolve these choices from the subject so each page has its own art direction.

## Set the art direction

- Before styling, identify two or three visual cues from the topic: its environment, material, energy, geometry, or central metaphor. Translate those cues into the canvas, palette, type, and shape language.
- Treat the background as part of the explanation's visual thesis. A precise subject may suit a clean solid field, an immersive technical subject a dark field, a layered subject tonal regions, a spatial system a restrained grid or node field, and a physical or human subject a fitting material texture.
- Carry the chosen canvas treatment through the page with enough variation to mark sections and enough continuity to feel like one authored work.

## Compose with intent

- Give the page one dominant visual idea and a clear reading rhythm. Use a strong hero, generous whitespace, and supporting callouts at a clearly subordinate scale.
- Use asymmetry or controlled overlap when it clarifies hierarchy. Keep alignment deliberate and preserve breathing room around labels, paths, and controls.
- Use a bento grid when the content is genuinely modular and peers deserve equal weight. Use a directional composition when the lesson has a narrative path.

## Let type do real work

- Pair expressive display typography with calm, highly readable body text. Create hierarchy through scale, weight, width, and spacing before adding containers.
- Keep display effects focused on moments of emphasis while body copy remains settled and immediately readable.

## Draw flows as flows

- For movement, exchange, cycles, and handoffs, favor continuous paths with smooth curves, generous bend radii, and rounded joins. Route them through whitespace and make every endpoint visibly connect.
- Use straight or orthogonal lines when they better express a boundary, measurement, comparison, or rigid system. Let connector geometry follow the relationship.
- Preserve differences in direction, payload, trust, state, and outcome using the connector grammar in the diagram guide.

## Build a coherent palette and depth

- Build the palette from the subject and assign colors semantic roles. Light neutral, dark, chromatic, and section-tinted canvases are all valid when they support the lesson and maintain contrast.
- Create depth with position, overlap, tonal contrast, shadows, and translucent layers calibrated to the hierarchy. Maintain readable contrast and obvious boundaries.
- Give each gradient, texture, translucent surface, border, and shadow a clear job in hierarchy, atmosphere, material, or interaction feedback.

## Add motion where it earns attention

- Use short ease-out transitions, path-following markers, or state changes to guide attention and explain what happens. Keep most of the page still so meaningful motion is noticeable.
- Prefer `transform` and `opacity`, preserve the complete static explanation, and honor `prefers-reduced-motion`.

The result should feel authored for this subject. Accuracy, accessibility, and rendered QA lead every aesthetic choice.
